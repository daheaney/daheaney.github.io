title:          "Trends in California CO<sub>2</sub> Emissions from Transportation and Building Energy seen through Independent Atmospheric Observations and a Bayesian Inversion"

pub:            "American Geophysical Union Annual Meeting"

abstract: >-
  Atmospheric observations of CO<sub>2</sub> provide an independent estimate of the efficiency of the myriad of public and private actions affecting greenhouse gases (GHGs) and climate. Here we analyze CO<sub>2</sub> observations through a Bayesian inversion to report anthropogenic CO<sub>2</sub> emissions for California’s two largest metropolitan regions: the San Francisco Bay Area and Los Angeles County. In the SF Bay Area, our 8-year record (2018 - 2025) affirms an ongoing trend of decreasing emissions, linked to transportation electrification and more fuel efficient gasoline powered vehicles. In LA, where we have a 4 and a half year record (2021-2025), emissions are also decreasing, yet at a slower rate. We find that in these regions nearly one-fifth of the annual emissions are from heating, hot water, and cooking in buildings using onsite natural gas. We observe CO<sub>2</sub> emissions attributable to this sector have remained relatively constant, as have economic reports of gas sales, suggesting slower decarbonization progress relative to transport. If these cities would like to meet the California state goal of net zero GHG emissions by 2045, more aggressive new policies will need to be implemented in the building energy sector.
