    <h2>Research</h2>
    <hr />
    <p>
        Every day, burning fossil fuels for transport, heating, and energy emits carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>) into the atmosphere, driving global climate change. To mitigate the worst impacts, the state of California has set an ambitious plan to reduce greenhouse gas emissions to net zero by 2045. Meeting that goal requires developing a clear picture of where emissions are decreasing and where progress has stalled.
    </p>
    <p>
        My research shows that yearly CO<sub>2</sub> emissions in the two largest urban areas of California, the San Francisco Bay Area and Los Angeles County, are decreasing. Over recent years, rising vehicle efficiency and growing electric vehicle adoption have driven this dropping trend. Unfortunately, emissions from homes and businesses burning natural gas for heating have not changed significantly for the past 4 years in LA and 8 years in the Bay Area.
    </p>
    <p>
        If these urban areas would like to meet the California state goal of net zero GHG emissions by 2045, more aggressive new policies will need to be implemented in the building energy sector. 
    </p>
